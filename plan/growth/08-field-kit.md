# Growth · the field kit

## Book · fk · The field kit · written for this plan, from the research it cites

What the books leave out and an SDE-2 search needs: sleep, movement, burnout, nerves, referrals, the resume, reputation at work, and the offer call. Each is short and practical; follow the links in the plan for more.

### fk-01 · Sleep is where learning sets
- **Group:** Body
- **Ask:** You study two hours past midnight to finish a design. How much of it will you remember next week?
- **Lesson:** Less than if you had slept. Memory is consolidated during sleep: deep sleep replays and stabilises what you learnt in the day, and REM sleep links it to what you already know. A night of short sleep cuts the next day's ability to form new memories and to focus. Sleep is not lost study time; it is the second half of studying. Aim for seven to nine hours, at a regular time (a regular wake time matters most). The plan's rule 6 says 7+ hours for this reason.
- **Card:** Why is sleep part of studying? ‖ Memory is consolidated in sleep (deep sleep stabilises, REM links); short sleep hurts tomorrow's learning and focus. Seven hours or more, regular wake time.
- **Try:** Set one fixed wake-up time for this week and keep it, weekends included.
- **Related:** dw-04
- **Source:** Diekelmann & Born (2010), “The memory function of sleep”, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* 11(2).

### fk-02 · Move your body
- **Group:** Body
- **Ask:** A 20-minute walk before a study block: a waste of study time or an investment?
- **Lesson:** An investment. Aerobic exercise raises BDNF, a protein that helps new neural connections form, and a single bout of moderate exercise improves attention and mood for a while after. Regular exercise improves sleep, lowers anxiety and protects against burnout. You don't need a gym: a brisk 20 to 30 minute walk most days is enough to matter (rule 6). Walk with a problem in your head and it is also productive meditation (dw-04).
- **Card:** Why exercise during an intense study plan? ‖ It raises BDNF (helps the brain form connections), sharpens attention and mood for hours, improves sleep, lowers anxiety. A brisk 20 to 30 minute walk most days is enough.
- **Try:** Stack a 20-minute walk on an existing habit: "After lunch on weekdays, I walk for 20 minutes."
- **Related:** dw-04
- **Source:** Szuhany, Bugatti & Otto (2015), a meta-analysis of exercise and BDNF, *Journal of Psychiatric Research* 60.

### fk-03 · Burnout: catch it early
- **Group:** Mind
- **Ask:** You have done six days straight, you're tired and slightly cynical about the whole plan. Push through or stop?
- **Lesson:** Burnout has three marks: **exhaustion**, **cynicism** (detachment, "what's the point") and a sense of **inefficacy** (feeling you are getting nowhere). It builds when demands stay high and recovery stays low for weeks. Early signs: irritability, dread before the session, poor sleep, needing more time for the same work. The fix is recovery, not more willpower: a real rest day (rule 5, the rest note), sleep, people, and cutting extras first (rule 8). Two days of real rest cost far less than two weeks of burnout.
- **Card:** The three marks of burnout, and the fix? ‖ Exhaustion, cynicism, inefficacy. Fix it with real recovery (a rest day, sleep, people, cutting extras), not more willpower.
- **Try:** Write down your own two early warning signs, and what you will do the first time you see them.
- **Source:** Maslach & Leiter (2016), “Understanding the burnout experience”, *World Psychiatry* 15(2).

### fk-04 · Nerves before an interview
- **Group:** Mind
- **Ask:** Your heart is pounding before a mock. Should you tell yourself to calm down?
- **Lesson:** Probably not. In Alison Wood Brooks's studies, people who said "I am excited" before a stressful performance did better than those who tried to calm down. Anxiety and excitement are the same high-arousal state; it is easier to relabel it than to suppress it (**reappraisal**). Then slow your breathing with long exhales (box breathing: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4) to steady your voice. Build a pre-interview ritual (water, notes, the first line of your intro out loud) so the start runs on autopilot. Practise in conditions like the real thing: mocks with strangers.
- **Card:** What should you tell yourself when nervous before an interview, and why? ‖ "I am excited." Anxiety and excitement are the same arousal; relabelling works better than calming down. Then slow breathing and a pre-interview ritual.
- **Try:** Before your next mock, say "I'm excited" out loud and do one minute of box breathing.
- **Plan:** Mocks start in week 8: [Interview prep](/weeks/interview).
- **Related:** ah-13, law-39
- **Source:** Brooks (2014), “Get excited”, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 143(3).

### fk-05 · Referrals: the message that works
- **Group:** Career
- **Ask:** Which gets a reply: "Hi, can you refer me?" or something else?
- **Lesson:** A referral is the single biggest boost to getting an interview at most companies. Make it easy and worth their while (hf-03, inf-07). A good message is short and specific: **shared identity** (same college, ex-colleague), **why this company and team**, **one line of proof** (what you built), and **the exact ask** with everything they need (job link, resume). Make it easy to say no. Follow up once, a week later. Thank them whatever happens, and tell them how it went.
- **Card:** The four parts of a referral message? ‖ Shared identity, why this team, one line of proof, and an exact, easy ask with the job link and resume (and a no that is easy to say).
- **Try:** Draft your referral message now: "Hi ___, fellow NIT Durgapur alum here. I'm applying for SDE-2 on ___ because ___. I built ___. Would you be open to referring me? Job link and resume attached; totally fine if not."
- **Plan:** Week 13: [ask for referrals](/weeks/13#w13-06).
- **Source:** Written for this plan, from How to Win Friends (principle 3) and Influence (“Unity”).

### fk-06 · The resume: impact first
- **Group:** Career
- **Ask:** "Worked on payment integration with Adyen" or "Built the Adyen integration that moved ₹X crore a month with zero double charges". Which gets the interview?
- **Lesson:** Recruiters skim a resume in seconds. Each bullet should lead with **impact**, then **what you did**, then **how**: an action verb, a number, the technology. (Google's formula: accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z.) Put your strongest story at the top (the plan says: the UCP story and the capstone). One page, no skills soup, links to the capstone repo and a short write-up. LinkedIn's headline should say what you want next, not your current title.
- **Card:** The formula for a resume bullet? ‖ Accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z: impact first, a number, then the how. Strongest story at the top, one page.
- **Try:** Rewrite your top three resume bullets in X-Y-Z form.
- **Plan:** Week 13: [the resume task](/weeks/13#w13-05).
- **Related:** hf-20
- **Source:** The X-Y-Z formula: Laszlo Bock, Google’s head of people operations, in a 2014 LinkedIn post on resumes.

### fk-07 · Reputation at work: be visible on purpose
- **Group:** Career
- **Ask:** Who knows what you shipped this quarter, besides your team?
- **Lesson:** Good work that nobody sees doesn't build a career (law 6). Keep a **brag document**: a running list of what you did, with the impact and the numbers, updated weekly. It makes self-reviews, promotions and interview stories easy. Make work visible: write short posts about what you shipped, demo it, give credit to others (it raises yours, hf-02). Your reputation is built in small consistent moments: answering clearly, delivering what you said, owning mistakes fast (hf-12).
- **Card:** What is a brag document, and why keep one? ‖ A weekly running list of what you did, with impact and numbers. It feeds reviews, promotions and interview stories, and makes your work visible.
- **Try:** Start your brag document today with three things from the last month.
- **Source:** The brag document: Julia Evans, “Get your work recognized: write a brag document” (2019).

### fk-08 · The offer call
- **Group:** Career
- **Ask:** The recruiter calls with an offer. What should you say in the first minute?
- **Lesson:** Thank them warmly and say you're excited, then ask for it **in writing** with the full breakdown (base, bonus, stock, joining bonus, level). Do not accept or negotiate on the first call: "I'd like to review it and get back to you by Thursday." Then prepare: your target and walk-away (ns-06), your concession plan (ns-09), your accusation audit (ns-03), and your other processes (law 20). On the negotiation call: open friendly (hf-13), anchor with data, ask calibrated questions (ns-07), stop at your target (law 47). Get the final version in writing.
- **Card:** The first minute of an offer call? ‖ Thank them, say you're excited, ask for it in writing with the full breakdown, and set a date to come back. Never accept or negotiate on the first call.
- **Try:** Write your offer-call checklist now: target, walk-away, concession steps, the non-cash ask.
- **Plan:** Week 13: [know your number](/weeks/13#w13-06).
- **Source:** Written for this plan, from Never Split the Difference, chs. 6 and 9.
